You bought the plush bed, placed it in the middle of the living room, and your cat sleeps on the delivery box instead. She's not ungrateful — she's a cat, and cats evaluate real estate differently than we do. The good news is that fixing it costs almost nothing.

Before you buy anything else, it helps to understand the four things a cat looks for in a napping spot: escape routes, height, warmth, and low traffic. Once you see the world through those criteria, the mystery of the ignored bed solves itself.

## What Cats Are Really Looking For

A bed in the center of a busy room fails every test a cat cares about. There's no quick exit, no height advantage, and people walk past constantly. Security beats softness every single time — which is why a $2 box with good sightlines will win against a $150 heated bed with none.

<ImageText src="/images/cat-cozy-spots-that-work/4.jpg" alt="A cat curled up sleeping on a soft blanket" side="left" aspect="4/3">
A bed against a wall, tucked under a chair, or inside an open closet reads as safe. Keep the fancy bed — just move it somewhere a nervous cat can watch the room with her back covered.
</ImageText>

## The Cardboard Box Effect

Why boxes? They're enclosed, they insulate, they smell like the house, and they give a cat something solid to press against. Paper bags work the same way. Cats will pick these over expensive furniture even when they've never been taught to — it's hardwired behavior, not a preference for recycling.

<Callout type="note" title="Free wins">
Keep a box around for a few days after a delivery. When it gets chewed, squished, or soggy, swap in a fresh one — replacement costs nothing.
</Callout>

## Height, Warmth, and Routine

The second winning formula is a spot that's up high and warm. A window perch, the top of a cat tree, or a shelf near a radiator gives a cat both security and a sunbath. Cats also revisit places where they've napped safely before, so a spot only becomes a favorite after a few successful naps there.

<ArticleImage src="/images/cat-cozy-spots-that-work/3.jpg" alt="A cat sitting on a cat tree looking out a window" variant="wide" aspect="16/9" credit="Photo: Pexels" />

Don't rearrange the furniture once you find a winning corner. Move it to clean, but put it back in the same place — cats are creatures of habit, and familiarity is part of the comfort.

## Make the Good Spots Work for You

You don't need to buy anything new. Put a soft blanket with your scent in the box, shift the fancy bed into a quiet corner near a window, and keep a second nap spot in a room where your cat can watch you safely. That combination covers every cat priority at once.

<ArticleImage src="/images/cat-cozy-spots-that-work/6.jpg" alt="A cat stretching on a sunny windowsill" variant="spotlight" aspect="16/9" caption="Warmth plus height plus a view: the trifecta your cat is looking for." credit="Photo: Pexels" />

<Steps title="A five-minute cozy upgrade">
<Step>Drop a blanket with your scent into the cardboard box.</Step>
<Step>Move the fancy bed to a quiet corner near a window.</Step>
<Step>Wash pet bedding with fragrance-free detergent once a month.</Step>
<Step>Keep a high perch in your main room so she can nap while watching you.</Step>
</Steps>

Watch where your cat actually sleeps for the next week, then move the furniture to match her. When the environment fits cat logic, the expensive bed usually gets used after all — just in the corner where she always wanted it.
